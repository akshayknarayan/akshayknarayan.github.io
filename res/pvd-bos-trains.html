Decision simulation: switch to MBTA, or stick with Amtrak?

For each historical northbound Amtrak trip, we look at what was knowable at a decision point M minutes before its scheduled Providence departure (the latest prediction available by then), then compare its actual arrival at South Station against the next MBTA Commuter Rail train's actual arrival. Positive "time saved" means switching to MBTA would have gotten you to Boston sooner.

Methodology notes. A trip's arrival is only counted once "settled": the poller kept polling that station at or after the scheduled/predicted time, so the last live prediction is treated as the actual. Trips still in progress as of the most recent poll are excluded rather than counted with an artificially small delay.

MBTA schedule-pattern trip IDs recur daily, so trips are re-keyed by (train id, service date) throughout — otherwise two different days' occurrences of the "same" trip id would collide.

Sample sizes are modest (a few weeks of data) and uneven across buckets — check the n column before trusting a percentage built on a handful of trips.
